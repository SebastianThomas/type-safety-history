# Corpus policy

## Purpose

The corpus records published results that connect a static type discipline to
an operational or semantic safety property. It is an evidence graph, not merely
a bibliography and not a claim that every relevant proof has been found.

## Included

- Progress, preservation and subject-reduction results.
- “Well-typed programs do not go wrong” results.
- Semantic, logical-relations, reducibility and step-indexed soundness proofs.
- Mechanized proofs and formal artifacts.
- Corrections, counterexamples and repaired proofs.
- Earlier precursors when their relationship to type safety is supported by a
  primary source.

## Initially excluded

Type-inference soundness, compiler correctness and logical consistency are not
classified as type safety unless a work explicitly connects the result to
runtime behavior. They may be linked as related results.

## Evidence standard

Every approved proof claim must identify a primary publication and a precise
document location. Claims about priority or “first” results require explicit
curator review and corroboration. Machine output is always a candidate.

The initial chronological anchors are Milner's 1978 semantic soundness result
and Wright and Felleisen's 1994 journal presentation of the syntactic approach.
The latter is a canonical modern anchor, not an unreviewed priority claim.
